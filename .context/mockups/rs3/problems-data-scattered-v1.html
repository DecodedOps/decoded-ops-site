The numbers never agree

Your supplier data is in six places, and none of them agree.

Ask what stock you actually hold of a specific SKU and you’ll get six different answers, depending who you ask and which spreadsheet, feed, or screen they’re looking at.

None of them are lying. They’re just all out of date in a different way, which is a product data management problem.

Talk about your situation See how the audit works
The symptoms

Six places, six different answers.

Every one of these is trying to describe the same catalogue. None of them talk to each other, so none of them are right for long.

  1. 01

    Supplier feeds. Updated on the supplier’s schedule, not yours, stock and pricing already stale by the time it lands.

  2. 02

    Spreadsheets. Whoever edited it last wins. No audit trail, no way to know which version is current.

  3. 03

    The ERP. Only as accurate as the last manual re-key, and re-keying six sources by hand doesn’t happen consistently.

  4. 04

    The website. Shows what was true when someone last remembered to update it, not what’s true right now.

  5. 05

    The warehouse floor. The only place stock is actually counted by hand, and the last place any system asks.

  6. 06

    Someone’s private list. The one person who “just knows” what’s really in stock. It works until they’re on holiday.

Count the ones you run. Each is a version of the catalogue that nobody reconciles.

Where the answers differ

The shelf, the aisle and the count that never match.

Three of my own photographs from client visits this year. The racking aisle, the bays counted by hand and the garments counted on a Friday are the places the numbers come from, and the places they disagree.

Why it happens

The data was never supposed to have one home.

Four decisions, each sensible on its own, that leave the same product with six descriptions.

01

Every system was added for its own job

The ERP for orders, the spreadsheet for the one supplier who doesn’t do feeds, the website plugin for the shop, each one solved its own problem and nobody planned how they’d stay in sync.

02

Syncing is a manual job nobody owns

Someone has to notice a mismatch and fix it by hand. When they’re busy, which is always, the mismatch just sits there.

03

No consistent SKU across sources

The same product has a different code in the supplier feed, the ERP, and the website. Matching them is treated as a one-off project instead of an ongoing job.

04

Nobody fully trusts any of them

So everyone double-checks against their own private version, which creates a seventh source of truth, and a seventh version of wrong.
