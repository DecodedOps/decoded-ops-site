Straight answer

When it’s the right call, and when it isn’t.

It fits when

You’ve grown past the point where you can hold operations in your head, and a full-time COO would be more than you need. You’ve just finished an audit and somebody has to own the plan. Or the person who held all of this has left, and you don’t want that to happen twice.

It doesn’t fit when

You need someone on site every day, which is a full-time hire. Or you haven’t worked out what’s actually broken yet, and a Clarity Audit comes first. I’d rather tell you that now than take a retainer for six months of finding out.

Questions

What owners ask me.

What is a fractional COO?

A part-time chief operating officer. They own how your operations run, from production flow to suppliers to the weekly management rhythm, for an agreed scope and cadence instead of a full-time salary.

Is a fractional COO the same as a fractional operations director?

In practice, yes. The titles get used interchangeably. What matters is that one person owns operations for you, with the scope and cadence agreed at the start.

How does a fractional COO engagement with you start?

With a Clarity Audit. I spend a day on site, then give you a written plan. If ongoing leadership is the right next step, it moves to Retained, which runs on a 12-month minimum, then rolling monthly.

When is a fractional COO the wrong choice?

When you need someone on site every day, or when you haven’t worked out what’s actually broken yet. The first is a full-time hire. The second is a Clarity Audit.
