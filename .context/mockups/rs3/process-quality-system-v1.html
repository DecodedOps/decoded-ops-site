The loop

The same four steps, for every process that matters.

Not a one-off audit. A loop that keeps running after the engagement ends, because the last step feeds the first one.

  1. Step 1

    Map the process

    As it actually runs, watched from start to finish. Not as the org chart describes it and not as anyone remembers it.

  2. Step 2

    Document it as an SOP

    Written so someone who doesn’t already know the job can follow it. That’s the test: not whether the person who wrote it recognises it.

  3. Step 3

    Check it’s followed

    Knowing how to write a standard operating procedure is only the start. An SOP nobody follows is a document, not a system. This is the step most businesses skip and the reason their documentation goes stale.

  4. Step 4

    Log what’s improved

    What changed, what it was worth, and what’s next. The log is yours, and it’s what makes the loop run again without me.

Then round again, for every process that matters.

Where the SOP gets tested

At the bench, on the shelf, at the door.

Three of my own photographs from client visits this year. A process is whatever happens at the bench and the door, not what the org chart says, and that’s where an SOP is checked.

What you keep

Three documents, and the habit of using them.

Built alongside the work, not written up afterwards. Each one is plain enough that your team keeps it going once I’ve gone.

The Process Register

Every process that matters, named, owned and dated, with where it is in the loop: mapped, documented, being checked, improved.

02

One-page SOPs
