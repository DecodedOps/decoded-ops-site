Where the cost hides

The problems that tend to appear

These are the patterns I see most often in operations consultant engagements. They look different in every business, but the underlying structure is almost always the same.

01

No one owns the technology decisions

Vendor choices and system changes get made without a plan. Every short-term fix creates a bigger problem later, and the cost quietly grows until it shows up.

02

Growing faster than your infrastructure

Revenue is growing, but your systems are straining. Manual workarounds that worked when you were smaller now create daily friction, and your team quietly absorbs the extra work.

03

Translating between your business and your technology

You shouldn't have to become technical to get the right outcomes from your systems. Without someone who bridges both worlds, things get lost between what you asked for and what got built.

04

Vendor decisions made without independent advice

Software salespeople are good at their job. Without someone on your side who knows what you actually need, you end up with tools that solve the vendor's problem, not yours.

I work with decorated-goods businesses across West Sussex, East Sussex, and Surrey. The operational problems in this trade don't change from postcode to postcode: supplier data that doesn't match what's on the shelf, artwork stuck in an email thread, and stock nobody fully trusts. An on-site day here works the same way it does everywhere: I follow the actual process, then write down what I find.

Whether a decoration business is based in Worthing itself or out towards Shoreham-by-Sea, Brighton, or Guildford, the same systems problems turn up: manual handoffs, disconnected data, and processes that live in someone's head rather than on paper.

The work I do is the same wherever the business is based: a structured, independent look at what's running, what it costs, and what it's holding back. But the conversation starts with understanding what's specific to this business, in this market.
