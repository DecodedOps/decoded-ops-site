Buy or build

It covers 25% of what you need. Do you buy it?

This gets asked in different words most weeks, usually framed as buy or build. It isn't really that question.

If something covers a quarter of the job, the decision isn't about the quarter. It's about the other three quarters, and what closing them costs. The three questions below work as an ERP evaluation checklist.

Before you buy anything

Three questions that replace the binary.

None of these require naming a platform. They work for whichever one you're looking at.

  1. 01

    Coverage. What percentage does it genuinely cover? If the best thing on the market covers a quarter of the job, the question isn't whether to buy it. It's what closes the other three quarters, and what that costs. Usually nobody has priced it, so it gets treated as free. It isn't free, it's just invisible.

  2. 02

    Fit. Does it work how you work? Or do you rework the business to fit the software? Every platform encodes an assumption about how you should operate. Fine if you agree with it. Expensive if you don't, and the cost never appears on the quote.

  3. 03

    Bloat. How much of it will you actually use? Platforms are built to cover a hundred and one scenarios because they have to sell to everyone. You'll use maybe a fifth of it. You pay for all of it, and you carry the complexity of the rest for as long as you own it.

Answer all three in writing before you look at a quote.

What the platform has to cover

The whole job, not the demo.

Three photographs from client sites. Machines mid-run, a racking aisle and packed cartons: the start-to-finish workflow a platform has to cover before its percentage means anything.

The arithmetic

Three numbers, and you can work them out yourself.

Nobody needs to sell you this. Follow your own processes and the numbers for your ERP evaluation checklist are already there.

Step 1

What it covers

Follow the workflow start to finish. Every point where the platform doesn't fit is part of the uncovered percentage.

Step 2

What the gap costs

Closing it with more software, or with someone's time every week. Both are real costs. Price whichever one you'd actually choose.

Step 3

What changing to fit it costs

What you'd have to rework about how the business runs to use the platform as designed. That number is usually the one nobody wrote down.

Sometimes the answer is buy. High coverage and a low cost to change how you work means buy it, clearly and quickly. This has to be able to come out that way, or it isn't a real test.

The options

Three ways to close the gap.

The platform covers what it covers. What closes the rest is more software, someone’s time every week, or a layer built for the gap. Each one has a price; the test is pricing all three.
